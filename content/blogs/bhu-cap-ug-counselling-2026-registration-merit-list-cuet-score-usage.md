# BHU CAP UG Counselling 2026: Registration, Merit List & CUET Score Usage

**Key Takeaways**

- BHU UG Admission 2026 registration for the Combined Allotment Program (CAP-UG) opened July 9, 2026, with the online application window closing **July 25, 2026**, at 11:59 PM on bhucuet.samarth.edu.in.
- **CUET UG 2026 normalised score is the sole merit criterion** for BHU UG admissions — there's no separate BHU entrance test or interview for most programmes.
- The first CAP-UG merit list is expected in **August 2026**, released in phases as seats are allotted round by round.
- Registration fee is ₹500 for UR/OBC-NCL/EWS and ₹250 for SC/ST/PwD candidates, paid online.
- CAP-UG is a fully online allotment process — no in-person reporting or offline counselling is involved.

<h2 id="what-is-cap-ug">What Is BHU CAP UG Counselling?</h2>

CAP-UG (Combined Allotment Program for Undergraduates) is Banaras Hindu University's centralised, fully online counselling system for allotting undergraduate seats to CUET UG 2026 qualified candidates. It replaces any separate BHU entrance exam or campus-level interview process — your CUET UG normalised score, preference list, and category together determine which programme and college within BHU you get allotted.

For a university with as many affiliated colleges and combined courses as BHU, this single-window system matters: instead of applying separately to each department, you register once on the CAP-UG portal, list your preferences in order, and let the system allot seats automatically based on merit.

> **EduNext tip:** BHU CAP UG has no upper limit on the number of preferences you can add. Add every eligible programme-college combination you'd consider — a longer preference list only improves your allotment chances, it never hurts them.

<h2 id="cuet-score-usage">How CUET UG Score Is Used for Merit</h2>

BHU UG admissions run entirely on your **CUET UG 2026 normalised score** — there is no additional BHU-specific test, and Class 12 marks play no independent role beyond meeting basic eligibility. Normalisation matters here because CUET is conducted across multiple shifts and dates; NTA's normalised score adjusts for difficulty variation between shifts so candidates across sessions are compared fairly.

Once you've registered on CAP-UG, the system uses three inputs together to decide your allotment:

| Factor | Role in Allotment |
|---|---|
| CUET UG 2026 normalised score | Primary merit ranking |
| Preference order (programme + college) | Determines which option is considered first |
| Category (UR/OBC/SC/ST/EWS/PwD, supernumerary quotas) | Applies reserved-seat eligibility |

If you're eligible under a supernumerary quota — BHU employee ward, sports quota, or similar — you must declare it explicitly during preference filling. Missing this declaration means you're processed only in the general merit pool, even if you qualified for a quota seat.

<h2 id="preference-filling">Preference Filling: The Step Most Candidates Get Wrong</h2>

After registration and fee payment, filling your programme-college preference list is the single most consequential step in the CAP-UG process. Preferences are processed strictly top-down: the system checks your highest preference first, and if your CUET score and category make you eligible, that's what gets allotted — regardless of whether a "better" option exists lower on someone else's list.

A few points worth internalising before you submit:

- **Order preferences by genuine willingness to join**, not just prestige — your top-ranked preference is what you'll likely get if you're anywhere near the cutoff for it.
- **Add every combination you'd accept.** There's no preference limit, and a shorter list only reduces your own options.
- **Declare quota eligibility upfront.** It cannot be added retroactively once allotment rounds begin.

> **Honest trade-off:** A long preference list feels tedious to fill out, but it's the only thing protecting you from an empty round if your top choices don't come through.

<h2 id="after-allotment">What Happens After Seat Allotment</h2>

BHU CAP UG merit lists are released in phases — the first list is expected in **August 2026**, with allotment continuing across further rounds depending on seat availability. Once you're allotted a seat, you generally have three choices:

1. **Freeze** — accept the current allotment and exit further rounds. Recommended if this is your first or second preference.
2. **Float** — accept the current seat but remain in the running for a higher preference in later rounds.
3. **Reject/withdraw** — decline the allotted seat, though this typically removes you from further consideration in that cycle.

Candidates accepting their first allotment must pay a commitment fee of **₹2,500 (₹500 for SC/ST candidates)** online to confirm the seat. The entire process — from registration to final allotment — is conducted online, with no offline counselling or in-person reporting required at any stage.


<h2 id="faqs">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

### Is there a separate entrance exam for BHU UG admission?

No. BHU UG admission runs entirely on your CUET UG 2026 normalised score through the CAP-UG portal — there's no additional BHU-specific test for most programmes.

### What is the last date to register for BHU CAP UG 2026?

The online registration window closes on **July 25, 2026, at 11:59 PM**, though candidates should confirm the exact date on bhuonline.in as schedules can shift.

### How many preferences can I add during BHU CAP UG counselling?

There is no upper limit. You can add as many eligible programme-college combinations as you want, and a longer list only improves your allotment chances.

### When will the BHU CAP UG merit list be released?

The first merit list is expected in **August 2026**, with subsequent rounds released as seats are allotted and vacancies are updated.

### What documents are needed for BHU CAP UG registration?

Class 10 and Class 12 marksheets, your CUET UG 2026 scorecard, category certificates (if applicable), and a photograph and signature in the prescribed format.
